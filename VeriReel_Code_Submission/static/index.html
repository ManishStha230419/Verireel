03 / SECURITY ENGINEERING

Treat every input as untrusted.

Security surrounds every comparison—from intake and network access to deletion and reporting. Each layer limits a different misuse or failure path.

01

Protected job access

Each comparison receives a high-entropy access credential. Only its browser session can poll, cancel, or download that job's report; the server stores only a SHA-256 token digest.

02

Hostile-media gate

Files must pass extension, container signature, size, duration, resolution, frame-rate, and readable-frame checks before fingerprint extraction.

03

Bounded resource use

Upload size, remote duration, active jobs, per-client work, request rate, download time, and free temporary storage are limited to reduce denial-of-service risk.

04

Restricted TikTok retrieval

Only HTTPS TikTok post hosts and allowlisted TikTok media hosts are accepted. Redirect targets and resolved network addresses are checked before fallback downloads.

05

Integrity and deletion

A SHA-256 digest is recorded before analysis. Source media is isolated per job and deleted on completion, cancellation, or error; result metadata expires from memory.

06

Safe, accountable output

Public errors omit internal details, security events avoid raw URLs and media, browser policies reduce injection exposure, and every similarity decision remains subject to human review.

07

Trusted network boundary

Host headers are allowlisted and forwarding headers are ignored unless an exact trusted-proxy path is configured, preventing spoofed client identities from bypassing rate controls.

08

Traceable security events

Every request receives a server-generated correlation ID. Security events use structured, rotating logs with pseudonymous clients and exclude access tokens, raw links, and source media.

09

Secure deployment profile

TLS is terminated by a production reverse proxy, with optional HSTS after HTTPS is verified. Detailed health capacity stays private unless a trusted operator explicitly enables it.

VIDEO COPY RESPONSE / NEPAL

Your video was copied. What now?

Start with evidence, choose the correct reporting route, and escalate only when the facts support it. A similarity score can organise a review, but it cannot prove ownership or infringement by itself.

  1. 01 / FREEZE

    Preserve both posts

    Save the original and suspected-copy URLs, usernames, captions, visible dates, view counts, and clear screenshots. Keep your original export, project files, raw footage, cloud history, and publishing records.

  2. 02 / CHECK

    Confirm your rights

    Check who created the footage, music, graphics, and edit; whether rights were assigned by contract; and whether the other post has permission or may rely on a lawful exception. A shared idea, trend, template, or fact is not automatically copying.

  3. 03 / REPORT

    Use TikTok's IP route

    For copied video—not impersonation—open the post and choose Share → Report → Counterfeits and intellectual property → Intellectual property violation, or use TikTok's copyright form. The claimant must be the owner or an authorised representative.

    Open TikTok copyright form ↗
  4. 04 / ESCALATE

    Act promptly in Nepal

    If platform reporting does not resolve a serious case, take the evidence to a Nepal-qualified lawyer and the appropriate police office. The Copyright Act states a three-month filing limit from knowledge of infringement, so do not delay.

MAIN LAW

Copyright Act, 2059 (2002)

प्रतिलिपि अधिकार ऐन, २०५९ is the main Nepal law for copied audiovisual content, supported by the Copyright Rules, 2061 (2004). Registration is optional; protection does not begin only after registration.

Official English Act ↗Nepal Law Commission text ↗Copyright Rules ↗
§§ 2–5

Videos can be protected works

The Act includes audiovisual works and protects original expression. Section 5 says registration is not compulsory, although voluntary registration may help organise ownership evidence.

§§ 7–8

Economic and moral rights

The rights holder controls reproduction, adaptation, distribution, exhibition, broadcast, and communication to the public. Authors also retain attribution and integrity interests described as moral rights.

§ 25

What may amount to infringement

Unauthorised reproduction, distribution, rental, or public communication can infringe protected rights, including when no profit is made. Permission, licence terms, ownership, and statutory exceptions still matter.

§ 27

Possible penalties and compensation

For a first offence, the Act states a fine of NPR 10,000–100,000, up to six months' imprisonment, or both. Repeat offences carry higher ranges; materials may be seized and compensation may be awarded.

§§ 32, 35, 37

Police and court route

The Act provides for a complaint to investigating police, possible seizure, District Court jurisdiction, state prosecution for Sections 27–28 cases, and investigation by an officer of at least Inspector rank.

§ 38

Three-month limitation

The Act says a suit must be filed within three months from learning of the infringement. Because limitation questions are fact-sensitive, obtain Nepal-qualified legal advice quickly.

EVIDENCE PACK

Bring facts, not only a score.

A clean evidence pack makes platform review, legal advice, and any police complaint easier to assess.

  • Original project files, raw footage, first export, and SHA-256 digest
  • Original and suspected-copy URLs, usernames, captions, and dates
  • Readable screenshots and a short chronological incident statement
  • Contracts, licences, permissions, invoices, or assignment documents
  • TikTok report reference, replies, and any takedown or appeal notices
  • VeriReel PDF as a review lead—not as proof or a legal conclusion

Legal boundary: not every similar or unauthorised-looking use is infringement. Nepal's Act includes limited permitted uses, and ownership or licence terms may change the answer. The Electronic Transactions Act, 2063 ↗ may be relevant to separate conduct such as unauthorised access, illegal electronic publication, or computer-related fraud; it should not automatically be applied to every copyright dispute. Evidence packs may contain personal information: follow Nepal's Privacy Act, 2075 ↗, send identity documents only through the official complaint channel, and use redacted copies in demonstrations. This guide is practical information, not legal advice.
